# CS255Portfolio
CS 255 Portfolio Reflection: DriverPass System Analysis and Design

Project Summary

The DriverPass project involved designing a web-based system for a driving school that wanted to help students better prepare for their driving tests. The client, DriverPass, wanted to combine online classes and practice tests with in-person instruction and on-the-road driving lessons. The system needed to support customer accounts, training packages, lesson scheduling, driver and vehicle assignments, online test tracking, employee account management, and reporting. My business requirements document identified the client's needs, while my system design document used UML diagrams and system requirements to explain how the proposed system would work.

What I Did Well

I believe I did particularly well at identifying the different functions the DriverPass system needed to support and organizing those needs into requirements and system designs. I focused on how customers and employees would interact with the system, including scheduling driving lessons, managing accounts, and accessing training resources. I also worked to represent system behavior through UML diagrams so the design would be easier for developers and other stakeholders to understand. Connecting the requirements to the system design helped me understand how the planning and design phases work together.

What I Would Revise

If I could revise one part of my work, I would improve the detail and consistency of my UML diagrams. I would review each diagram to ensure that all important workflows, decision points, and exception scenarios were represented clearly. For example, the lesson-scheduling process should account for situations such as an unavailable instructor or vehicle, a customer having no remaining driving hours, or a scheduling conflict. I would also verify that the diagrams align closely with the business requirements document so that every major client requirement is accounted for in the design.

Interpreting and Implementing User Needs

I interpreted the client's needs by focusing on the problems DriverPass wanted to solve and the tasks its customers and employees needed to complete. The system design addressed those needs through features such as online learning resources, practice tests, lesson reservations, account management, and reporting. I also considered different user roles because customers, the secretary, the IT officer, and the owner would need different levels of access and functionality.

Considering user needs is important because a system can be technically functional but still fail if it does not solve the actual problems users experience. Understanding requirements before designing a system helps prevent misunderstandings, reduces unnecessary rework, and ensures that the finished product provides value to the client. It also helps developers make informed decisions about usability, security, and system behavior.

My Approach to Software Design

My approach to software design is to begin by understanding the problem, identifying the stakeholders, and gathering clear requirements before deciding how the system should work. I would use questions, interviews, and requirement lists to clarify expectations and identify constraints. From there, I would use techniques such as UML use case, activity, and sequence diagrams to represent user interactions and system behavior. I would also identify alternate workflows and error conditions rather than focusing only on the ideal scenario.

In future projects, I would continue using documentation and diagrams to communicate designs, while reviewing the requirements throughout the process to ensure the design remains aligned with the client's goals. I would also seek feedback from stakeholders and teammates early so that misunderstandings can be corrected before implementation. This project helped me recognize that successful software development requires more than writing code; it depends on understanding user needs, communicating clearly, planning carefully, and designing a system that can be maintained and improved over time.
